Hi5 Portal: Expected Behavior for Subject Teachers and Section Assignment
Summary
In a school, a section has one adviser and many subject teachers. Before the school year
opens, the school sets up sections, names the adviser of each, and gives every subject in
each section a teacher (the class program and teaching loads). Grades are then encoded by
those teachers. The system should follow that same order. A grade belongs to one teacher,
one subject, and one section, and being the adviser does not by itself give grading rights.
Rules marked (decide) need a choice from the team, the Registrar, or the school head.
Assigning teachers in Section Creation
1. The Section Creation page (Create and Edit Section) lets the Administrator and the
Registrar assign teachers (decide: Registrar too). Besides Adviser, Capacity, and Min./Max.
Average, the section has a Subject Teachers part.
2. The Subject Teachers part lists every subject of the section's grade level and program,
taken from Subject Management. For STE and SPFL sections it includes their extra subjects.
For each subject there is a teacher dropdown. MAPEH shows Music, Arts, PE, and Health as
four lines. TLE shows one line with its specializations.
3. Each subject in a section has one teacher (decide: allow co-teachers). The same teacher
may be chosen for many subjects and many sections.
4. A teacher can be assigned to one subject across several sections at once (for example
Math 7 in 7-A to 7-F). This saves the Administrator from repeating the same choice.
5. Electives are not assigned here. They are assigned to the elective offering in Subject
Management, with no section, because the class is the learners who chose it.
6. The adviser is chosen in the same window. The adviser must be a teacher. One teacher
advises at most one section. The adviser may or may not teach in the advisory section.
7. The system shows a warning, not a block, when a teacher is assigned two classes that
overlap in the Master Schedule, or goes over the teaching load set by the school (decide the
maximum). The Administrator can still save.
8. A section with subjects that have no teacher is flagged, so the Administrator sees what is
missing before grading starts.
9. The assignments of a closed school year stay as history. A new school year starts with the
sections blank, with the option to copy the previous year's assignments for the Administrator
to edit (decide).
10. Every assignment or change is written to the Activity Log with the actor, section, subject,
old teacher, new teacher, and time.
What a subject teacher sees
11. Grade Management lists every section where the teacher is assigned at least one
subject, not only the advisory section. Each shows only the learners enrolled in it this school
year.
12. For a core subject the class list is the whole section. For an elective it is the learners who
chose that elective, from any section.
13. The class list, schedule, and at-risk lists show the same classes.
14. A teacher cannot open a section where he has no assignment and is not the adviser. A
direct link is refused with a permission message.
Grading
15. A teacher can encode, import, and submit grades only for the subject and section
combinations assigned to him. Other subjects in the same section are read-only or hidden
(decide).
16. The Excel template lists only the learners in the class. A row for a learner outside the
class is refused with a message such as "LRN is not on this class roster."
17. The server refuses any save for a subject or section that is not assigned, even if the
request is changed. The refusal is a permission-denied message (decide: log denied
attempts).
18. Grades lock when the teacher submits them. A locked cell cannot be edited. The teacher
files a correction request with a justification, and the Registrar approves it. After approval,
the teacher can re-enter grades only in the subjects he handles, and subjects handled by
other teachers stay read-only.
19. The General Average and the final grade are computed by the system and cannot be
edited.
The adviser
20. The adviser sees all learners in the advisory section and every subject's grades. The
adviser can edit only the subjects assigned to him. Subjects taught by others are read-only.
21. The adviser can see which subjects are missing grades and which teacher owns each
one, so he can follow them up.
22. The adviser can run bulk promotion for the advisory section once every subject has
grades for all three terms, and can generate SF1, SF5, SF9, and SF10 for it.
23. The adviser can save SF9 attendance and observed values only for learners in the
advisory section.
24. The adviser can tag a learner as Non-Reader (decide: only advisory learners, or any
learner he teaches).
What a teacher cannot do
25. A teacher cannot enroll learners, place them in sections, approve transfers, or change
teacher assignments.
When things change mid-year
26. If a teacher leaves or is replaced, the Administrator changes the assignment. The old
grades stay and the old teacher can no longer edit them. The new teacher can edit them
(decide).
27. If a learner transfers to another section, the new section's teachers see the learner, and
the old section's teachers do not.
Records
28. Assignment changes, grade saves, locks, corrections, and approvals are written to the
Activity Log with the actor, learner, subject, old value, new value, and time.
